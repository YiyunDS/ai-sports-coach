Where the pro's numbers come from

The pro's knee bend (about 97°) and push (about 163°) were measured from a slow-motion clip of Connor McDavid. Its lean, the shape of its stride, its arm swing and elbow angles, how it holds its stick, and how its skates tip are still our estimates.

      How the chances work

      Nobody knows if one play will work. The game rolls dice, like real hockey: a short pass to an open teammate works almost every time, a long pass past a defender often does not. A shot from the slot scores far more often than a shot from the corner. Being in the right spot makes you open for a pass, and makes their shots harder. The numbers can be changed in js/gamesim.js.

      The two rules

      Offside. The puck must cross the other team's blue line before any attacker does. A player is offside when both skates are completely over the line before the whole puck is. One skate still on the line is fine, and the stick does not count.

      Icing. A player shoots the puck from their own half of the rink (behind the center red line) and it goes all the way over the far goal line with nobody touching it. It is not icing if it goes in the net, if the other team plays it first, or if it stops short.

      These are the USA Hockey rules for players 12 and under: the whistle blows right away, and a team that is short a player may not ice the puck. Sources are in SOURCES.md.
