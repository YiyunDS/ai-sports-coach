      Play-by-play

        How to play

        You skate one skater for the whole game. The computer plays your teammates, the other team and both goalies.

        Skate
        W A S D or the arrow keys. On a phone, a joystick appears under your left thumb.
        Pass
        - The pass goes to the teammate you are steering toward. A thin line shows who.
        Take
        Space Take the puck from the player who has it. You must be really close: about a stick's length. From the front works best, and it does not work every time.
        Shoot
        P Steer toward one side of the net to aim there.
        Call
        L When a teammate has the puck: "I am open, pass to me!"
        Move
        M A quick move past a defender. You are fast for a moment, then slow for a moment.

        The two bars

        A pass or a shot takes two taps. While you have the puck, a mark runs up and down the Power bar. The first tap stops it. Then a mark runs on the Height bar, and the second tap stops that one and sends the puck. Green is the good part. A soft pass is easy to pick off, and a very hard one is hard to catch. A high shot can beat the goalie, but too high goes over the net.

        The whistle

        Offside. Nobody on the attacking team may be inside the other team's blue line before the puck. That goes for you, your teammates and the other team. So look before you pass or carry the puck in. The player with the puck may cross ahead of it.

        Icing. A puck sent from your own half that goes all the way over the far goal line with nobody touching it. The face-off comes back to your end.

        Saves. When a goalie covers the puck the whistle blows. After a rebound there is no whistle: keep playing.

        Face-off. If you are the center, tap Pass the moment the puck drops. Too early and you lose it.

        After the game a coach's report appears under the rink: how well you held your position, your passes, your shots, your takes, and the one thing to work on next.

        Older age groups are harder: the other team is faster, the goalie stops more, and computer players go offside or ice the puck more often. Nobody knows if one shot will go in, so the game still rolls dice, like real hockey. The numbers can be changed in js/gamesim.js.

        The two rules

        Offside. The puck must cross the other team's blue line before any attacker does. A player is offside when both skates are completely over the line before the whole puck is. One skate still on the line is fine, and the stick does not count. It is the same when the puck is shot or passed in, and when it comes out of the zone and goes back in.

        Two surprises, at the Pro level: the player who has the puck may cross the line ahead of it, and it is not offside when the other team takes the puck back into its own zone.

        Icing. The puck leaves a stick in that team's own half of the rink (behind the center red line) and goes all the way over the far goal line. A shot or a long pass, it is the same. The last touch by that team is the one that counts. It is not icing if it goes in the net, if the other team plays it first, if a teammate catches it, or if it stops short.

        These are the USA Hockey rules for players 12 and under: the whistle blows right away, and a team that is short a player may not ice the puck. Sources are in SOURCES.md.
